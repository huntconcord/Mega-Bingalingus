## Background

The Cholas were an old Tamil royal dynasty, but their power had declined before the ninth century.

Vijayalaya Chola revived the dynasty around the middle of the 800s by capturing Thanjavur in the fertile Kaveri River region. His successor Aditya I expanded Chola power by defeating the Pallavas and taking much of their territory.

By 900, the Cholas controlled an increasingly powerful kingdom in southern India. However, the enormous imperial conquests and overseas expeditions associated with Rajaraja I and Rajendra I belonged to the late tenth and eleventh centuries.

## Culture
![[Pasted image 20260922115014.png]]![[Pasted image 20260922115049.png|267]]

Chola culture developed from Tamil language, literature, religion, agriculture, and political traditions.

Hinduism was dominant. Chola rulers especially supported Shaivism, the worship of Shiva, while also patronizing other Hindu traditions. Buddhist and Jain communities remained present.

Temples functioned as:

- Religious centers
- Landowners
- Employers
- Banks and treasuries
- Centers of music and festivals
- Administrators of agricultural resources

Tamil was the principal regional language, while Sanskrit remained important in religion and elite scholarship.

The Kaveri delta supported intensive rice cultivation. Control of its irrigation systems, villages, temples, and agricultural surplus gave the early Cholas the resources needed for expansion.

## Military
![[Pasted image 20260922115126.png]]

### Organization

The highly organized imperial regiments known from later Chola inscriptions were still developing around 900.

Early Chola armies probably included:

- Royal household troops
- Infantry supplied by local authorities
- Noble retainers
- Archers
- Spearmen
- Cavalry
- War elephants
- Allied contingents
- Troops provided by subordinate rulers

The king or a royal prince could personally lead campaigns. Military service was tied to loyalty, landholding, prestige, and shares of captured wealth.

### Gear and equipment
![[Pasted image 20260922120012.png|306]]

Chola infantry commonly used:

- Spears
- Bows
- Straight or slightly curved swords
- Large knives
- Javelins
- Axes
- Round or elongated shields

Elite soldiers may have worn metal helmets, mail, scale armor, or layered textile protection. Ordinary infantry probably relied on shields and cloth protection.

Cavalrymen carried spears, swords, and bows. Horses were valuable because much of southern India was unsuitable for breeding large numbers of high-quality warhorses, making imported animals strategically important.

War elephants could carry a driver and one or more warriors equipped with bows, spears, or javelins.

### Tactics and fighting style
![[Pasted image 20260922115434.png|358]]

Early Chola armies used combined forces.

Likely tactics included:

- Archery before close combat
- Spearmen holding the main line
- Elephants attacking dense formations
- Cavalry striking exposed flanks
- Raiding agricultural land
- Capturing temples and political centers
- Pursuing defeated rulers
- Forcing subordinate chiefs to change allegiance

South Indian warfare often centered on possession of fortified cities, river valleys, temples, and productive farmland rather than permanent occupation of every settlement.

### Siege and naval warfare

Chola forces could attack fortifications with:

- Elephants used against gates
- Ladders
- Fire
- Blockades
- Battering equipment
- Mining or undermining
- Destruction of surrounding crops

The Cholas possessed coastal vessels and could transport troops by river or sea. However, the celebrated blue-water navy of the later Chola Empire should not be projected fully onto the kingdom of 900.

### Military quirks
![[Pasted image 20260922120318.png|357]]

Elephants were both military weapons and displays of kingship. Capturing an enemy ruler’s elephants, banners, royal insignia, or ceremonial objects carried enormous political significance.

Victories were commemorated through royal titles, temple gifts, inscriptions, and heroic poetry. Warfare was therefore closely tied to the public reputation of the king.

## Politics
![[Pasted image 20260922120941.png|326]]

The Chola king worked through princes, officers, local assemblies, temples, landholders, and subordinate chiefs.

Tamil villages possessed organized assemblies that managed:

- Irrigation
- Taxes
- Temple property
- Land disputes
- Local labor
- Communal resources

Royal government did not eliminate these institutions. Instead, the Cholas gradually connected them to a wider system of taxation, military service, and temple patronage.

By 900, the Cholas were an expanding regional kingdom rather than the dominant Indian Ocean empire they would later become.
