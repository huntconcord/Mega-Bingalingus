## Background

By the late 800s, Silla had lost control of many provinces. Heavy taxation, court conflict, peasant revolts, and powerful local strongmen fractured the kingdom.

Gyeon Hwon, a former Silla officer, established Later Baekje in 900.

Gung Ye established Later Goguryeo in 901. One of his commanders, Wang Geon, later overthrew him and founded Goryeo in 918.

Thus, in the exact year 900, Later Baekje existed, Silla was collapsing, and Later Goguryeo was immediately about to emerge.

## Culture

All three states inherited institutions and traditions from Silla and earlier Korean kingdoms.

Korean society was influenced by:

- Buddhism
- Confucian political ideas
- Chinese writing
- Clan identity
- Ancestor rituals
- Regional loyalties
- Earlier Goguryeo and Baekje traditions

Buddhist temples held land, educated monks, produced art, and sometimes possessed significant political influence.

The successor rulers deliberately invoked ancient names. Later Baekje and Later Goguryeo claimed to restore prestigious kingdoms from Korea’s past.

## Military

### Organization

The competing kingdoms depended on:

- Royal guards
- Provincial garrisons
- Local warlord armies
- Noble retainers
- Peasant levies
- Mounted warriors
- Archers
- Fortress troops
- Naval forces

Regional commanders frequently changed allegiance. A ruler’s success depended on rewarding officers and persuading local strongmen to join him.

### Gear and equipment

Korean soldiers used:

- Composite bows
- Crossbows
- Spears
- Swords
- Polearms
- Daggers
- Round or rectangular shields

Elite cavalry could wear lamellar or scale armor with iron helmets. Some horses may also have received protective armor.

Ordinary infantry generally possessed lighter equipment and depended heavily on bows, spears, and shields.

### Tactics and fighting style

Korean warfare combined archery, cavalry, infantry, and fortification.

Common tactics included:

- Massed archery
- Cavalry raids
- Spear formations
- Night attacks
- Ambushes in mountain passes
- Defending walled towns
- Capturing regional fortresses
- Cutting supply roads
- Encouraging enemy commanders to defect

Mountainous terrain made fortresses particularly important. Control of several strongholds could matter more than holding a continuous front line.

### Siege and naval warfare

Armies attacked fortifications using:

- Ladders
- Fire
- Battering equipment
- Mining
- Blockades
- Archery against defenders
- Assaults against gates

Naval forces moved troops and supplies along Korea’s coast and rivers. Wang Geon’s later rise was assisted by his family’s maritime experience and control of western coastal connections.

### Military quirks

Changing allegiance was a major part of warfare. Local commanders were often more concerned with protecting their own families and territory than preserving the weakening Silla monarchy.

The three states fought for political legitimacy as well as territory. Reviving the names Goguryeo and Baekje helped new rulers recruit populations with strong regional identities.

## Politics

Silla retained the ancient monarchy and court at Gyeongju but possessed increasingly little power outside its core territory.

Later Baekje was built around Gyeon Hwon’s personal authority and military followers. It became especially powerful in southwestern Korea.

Later Goguryeo was initially ruled by Gung Ye, who combined military power with increasingly extravagant religious and royal claims.

By 900, Korea was beginning a civil war between competing regional states. The conflict ended when Goryeo unified the peninsula in 936.
