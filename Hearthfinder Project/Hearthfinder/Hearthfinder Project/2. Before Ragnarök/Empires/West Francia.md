## Background

West Francia was created by the Treaty of Verdun in 843. It was given to Charles the Bald, a son of Louis the Pious and grandson of Charlemagne.

The kingdom included most of the western Frankish territories. Its population increasingly spoke Romance dialects descended from Latin, although Germanic languages remained present in some areas.

West Francia faced repeated Viking attacks. Viking fleets traveled inland along rivers such as the Seine and Loire, raiding monasteries, cities, farms, and royal estates.

The Viking siege of Paris in 885–886 demonstrated both the danger of the raids and the growing power of local nobles. Count Odo organized the city’s defense and later became king.

## Culture

West Francia preserved many Carolingian cultural traditions. Latin remained the language of the church, government documents, and scholarship.

West Frankish culture included:

- Catholic Christianity
- Monasteries and cathedral schools
- Carolingian art and manuscript production
- Roman legal traditions
- Germanic customs among the nobility
- Developing Romance languages

The Romance dialects spoken in West Francia gradually became more distinct from the Germanic languages of East Francia. These dialects eventually contributed to the development of Old French.

Monasteries remained important centers of literacy, wealth, and education. However, their valuable religious objects and relatively weak defenses made them frequent targets for Viking raiders.

## Military

### Organization

West Francia lacked a single permanent army. The king relied on:

- Royal household troops
- Mounted noble retainers
- Regional levies
- Soldiers supplied by counts
- Fortified towns
- Local defenders
- Church lands required to provide military support

Because Viking attacks happened quickly, local counts often responded before the king could assemble a royal army. This increased the political and military independence of regional nobles.

### Gear and equipment

West Frankish warriors used equipment inherited from the Carolingian military tradition.

Mounted nobles commonly carried:

- Spears or lances
- Straight swords
- Round or oval shields
- Javelins
- Daggers

Wealthier soldiers wore mail shirts and conical helmets. Ordinary infantry used spears, axes, bows, knives, and wooden shields.

### Tactics and fighting style

West Frankish warfare increasingly concentrated on defending settlements and responding to Viking raids.

Common tactics included:

- Rapid cavalry responses
- Guarding bridges and river crossings
- Fortifying important towns
- Constructing fortified bridges
- Blocking Viking ships from traveling upriver
- Protecting monasteries and royal estates
- Surrounding Viking camps
- Paying raiders to leave when military resistance failed

The Edict of Pistres, issued by Charles the Bald in 864, attempted to improve military readiness. It encouraged the construction of fortified bridges and required certain landowners to possess horses and military equipment.

Viking armies frequently avoided Frankish forces by returning to their ships. West Frankish cavalry could move rapidly on land, but it struggled to intercept fleets that could change direction along rivers.

### Siege and defensive warfare

West Francia constructed or reinforced:

- Town walls
- Castles and fortified residences
- River barriers
- Fortified bridges
- Watch posts
- Defended monasteries

During the Siege of Paris, the defenders used the city’s walls and fortified bridges to prevent Viking ships from continuing along the Seine.

### Military quirks

West Francia’s greatest military problem was its divided command. Counts were often better positioned to defend their own territories than the king, but their growing power weakened royal authority.

Kings sometimes paid Viking armies to leave. This could protect a region temporarily, but it also encouraged raiders to return in search of additional payments.

## Politics

Charles the Bald struggled against rival Carolingian rulers, rebellious nobles, and Viking attacks. Royal authority remained strongest in areas directly controlled by the king.

Regional counts increasingly treated their offices and lands as hereditary possessions. They constructed fortifications, maintained private troops, and sometimes ignored royal commands.

After Charles the Fat was removed from power in 887, West Frankish nobles elected Odo as king. His selection demonstrated that military ability and noble support could outweigh Carolingian ancestry.

Charles the Simple, a member of the Carolingian dynasty, became king in 898. By 900, West Francia remained a kingdom, but much of its practical authority belonged to powerful regional nobles.
