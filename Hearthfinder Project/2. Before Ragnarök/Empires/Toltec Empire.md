## Background / Historical caution

The Toltecs are appropriate only as an **emerging civilization** in 900. Tula existed before then, but its transformation into the major Toltec capital occurred around or after 900.

Much of the traditional Toltec story comes from later Aztec accounts that mixed history, political propaganda, and legend. Archaeologists therefore distinguish between the people who inhabited Tula and the idealized “Toltecs” remembered centuries later.

Tula’s chronology remains debated, but the city became a major political and ceremonial center during the Early Postclassic period.

## Culture

Toltec culture developed from the mixture of peoples and traditions that followed the decline of Teotihuacan and other Classic-period centers.

Tula displayed cultural connections with:

- Central Mexican traditions
- Northern migrant groups
- Earlier Teotihuacan culture
- Gulf Coast communities
- Maya regions
- Long-distance trading networks

Religion involved deities and supernatural forces associated with warfare, rain, fertility, fire, rulership, and the Feathered Serpent.

Tula contained:

- Stepped pyramids
- Ballcourts
- Columned halls
- Palaces
- Plazas
- Warrior sculptures
- Reliefs depicting animals and human hearts

## Military

### Organization

Evidence suggests an increasingly militarized society, but the exact structure of the Toltec army is unknown.

Toltec forces probably included:

- Elite warrior orders
- Noble retainers
- Commoner levies
- Atlatl-equipped missile troops
- Spearmen
- Archers
- Tributary allies

The famous Atlantean warrior sculptures at Tula represent an elite military ideal, although the surviving monuments largely reflect the civilization after 900.

### Gear and equipment

Toltec warriors used or are depicted with:

- Atlatls or spear-throwers
- Darts
- Spears
- Bows
- Stone-edged clubs
- Knives
- Round shields
- Quilted cotton armor

Obsidian blades produced exceptionally sharp cutting edges but could chip or break. Wooden weapons could be fitted with replaceable obsidian blades.

Elite warriors wore elaborate headdresses, pectorals, back ornaments, and military costumes. Such clothing communicated rank, religious affiliation, and membership in warrior groups.

### Tactics and fighting style

Likely tactics included:

- Atlatl volleys before close combat
- Archery
- Dense groups of spearmen
- Raids against settlements
- Ambushes
- Capturing enemies for sacrifice
- Intimidation through military display
- Forcing communities to supply tribute

Armies fought on foot. There were no horses or war elephants in pre-Columbian Mesoamerica.

Warfare could seek territory, tribute, trade access, captives, and political prestige rather than the complete destruction of an enemy population.

### Fortifications and siege warfare

Tula and other centers used defensible locations and substantial architecture. Siege methods probably included:

- Missile attacks
- Fire
- Ladders
- Blockades
- Assaults on entrances
- Cutting access to food or water

There is little evidence for mechanical siege engines comparable to those used in Eurasia.

### Military quirks

Toltec military imagery blended warfare and religion. Eagles, jaguars, coyotes, feathered serpents, and human hearts appeared in ceremonial art.

Capturing a prestigious enemy could be more valuable than killing him immediately because captives could be displayed, interrogated, exchanged, or sacrificed.

## Politics

The nature of the Toltec state remains debated. Tula may have governed a tributary network rather than a uniformly administered territorial empire.

Political authority probably combined:

- Sacred kingship
- Warrior elites
- Priestly institutions
- Tribute relationships
- Control of trade
- Alliances with neighboring communities

Later Aztec rulers claimed Toltec ancestry because “Toltec” became associated with legitimate rulership, refined art, and ancient civilization.

For a 900 AD setting, this faction should be portrayed as a new and aggressively expanding power, not the fully developed Toltec state of the eleventh century.
